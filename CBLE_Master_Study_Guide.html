1 · Customs business and the broker · Part 111

The definition that opens the exam (111.1)

IT IS customs business — done for others, requires a license: entry and admissibility of merchandise (including TIB); classification and valuation; payment of duties, taxes and fees; refunds and drawback; and preparing or signing documents for those activities intended for CBP.

It is NOT customs business — no license required: mere electronic transmission of data with no judgment over content; an importer acting on its own behalf; corporate compliance activity guiding affiliates of the same corporate group; marketing, freight and pure logistics.

Customs territory (101.1): the 50 states + District of Columbia + Puerto Rico. OUTSIDE: U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa and other territories.
Trap. The exam loves the negative side of the definition — wrong options dress up as "customs business" something that isn't, or the reverse. Also watch Puerto Rico (inside) versus USVI/Guam (outside).

License requirements — individual (111.11)

  • U.S. citizen — duration of citizenship is irrelevant
  • 21 years of age before applying
  • Not an officer or employee of the United States
  • Good moral character and reputation
  • 75% on the examination
  • Application on CBP Form 3124 + fee, filed within 3 years of passing

Firms and the permit

Partnership / corporation / association: licensed by having at least one licensed qualifying member or officer, who must exercise responsible supervision and control (111.28).

National permit: single and nationwide — enables customs business throughout the customs territory. The firm must designate a knowledgeable point of contact available to CBP during and outside normal operating hours.

Without a license (111.4): penalty of $10,000 per transaction or violation, capped at $30,000 total.

License ≠ permit. The license qualifies the person or firm; the national permit authorizes the practice. Losing the qualifying member does not automatically void the license — but there are reporting deadlines (see §4).

2 · Broker duties, records and discipline · Part 111

Conduct

  • Promptly transmit funds — CBP refund checks to the client, duties to CBP
  • Keep client funds in separate accounts
  • Do not withhold client documents to force payment of fees
  • Give the client written notice of debts owed to CBP
  • If a client refuses to correct a known error, the broker withdraws from the transaction and advises the client of the error
  • No fee-sharing with anyone whose license was suspended or revoked
  • Confidential client records: disclose only to the client, to CBP, or as ordered by a court — not to the surety, freight forwarder or other clients

Records (111.23)

Keep customs business records 5 years. Records transmitted to CBP must still be kept by the broker — CBP retaining a copy does not relieve the broker.

Cyber breach (111.21)

On discovering a breach of electronic or physical records relating to customs business, notify CBP SOC (Office of Information Technology Security Operations Center) electronically within 72 hours.

Discipline

Denial, suspension and revocation with notice and hearing. Monetary penalty up to $30,000 per violation. Suspension by operation of law (missed triennial report) is automatic and is cured by filing the report and fee — distinct from disciplinary suspension, which requires process.

Convicted felon employee. Knowingly employing a person convicted of a felony without CBP approval — the broker must request approval from the Executive Director, Office of Trade.

3 · Bonds · Part 113

The three parties

Principal (importer/broker — obtains the bond), Surety (guarantees, jointly and severally, but only up to the bond amount), CBP (beneficiary).

Types

  • Single Transaction Bond (STB) — one entry
  • Continuous bond — all entries for 1 year, auto-renewing. Activity Code 1 (importer) minimum generally $50,000 or 10% of duties/taxes/fees paid in the prior 12 months

Riders (113.24)

Amend an existing bond without reissuing it: name change, addition of trade name / co-principal, increase or decrease of amount. Requires signature of principal and surety, and CBP approval. A rider has its own effective date and does not retroact.

Rider ≠ new bond. A genuine change of legal identity — merger, succession, change of entity type — requires a new bond, not a rider. Termination (113.27) is different again: it ends the bond and has its own notice rule.

Inadequate bond

If CBP determines a bond is insufficient, the principal has 15 days from notification to cure (113.13).

6 · Entry and entry summary · Parts 141–143

The two distinct acts (141.0a)

EntryEntry summary
PurposeRelease the cargoDetermine duty; declare
FormCBP Form 3461CBP Form 7501
Deadline15 calendar days from arrival10 working days from time of entry
PaymentNo — secured by bondYes — deposit of estimated duties
Statute19 USC 1484(a)(1)(A)19 USC 1484(a)(1)(B)

Entry Summary Certified for Cargo Release — a single ACE transmission serving both functions. Under 141.68(a)(2) the time of entry is when the entry summary is filed in proper form with estimated duties deposited.

Right to make entry (141.11 & 141.12)

Acceptable evidence: bill of lading or air waybill properly endorsed; a certified duplicate bill; a shipping receipt or other document in lieu of a bill when entry is made by the actual consignee in person. Not acceptable: an extract from a bill that has not been certified as genuine by the carrier.

Lien. A carrier's or warehouseman's lien blocks release even where someone otherwise has the right to make entry. CBP does not arbitrate the debt — it simply does not release. Right to make entry ≠ right to take the goods.

Duty rate — which one applies (141.69)

The rate in effect on the date of entry, except:

  • Warehouse entry — the rate at the time of withdrawal
  • Immediate Transportation (IT) — the rate in effect when the IT entry was accepted at the port of original transport
  • Informal mail entry — when the CBP employee completes preparation of the entry documentation
Practical effect of the IT exception. On cargo moving in bond the rate freezes at IT acceptance in the origin port. A tariff change mid-route does not reach the shipment.

Invoices (141.81–141.86)

  • 141.81 — a commercial invoice is required
  • 141.83(d) — the situations where an invoice is not required
  • 141.86(a) — the information that must appear on each invoice; missing data may be entered manually
  • 141.86(d) — the invoice must be in English

Recall of merchandise (141.113(a))

Even after release, CBP may recall merchandise into its custody, using CBP Form 4647. Failure to redeliver → liquidated damages under the bond.

Commingled goods (152.13)

Goods of different rates packed together so that quantity/value of each class cannot be readily ascertained are dutiable at the HIGHEST rate applicable to any part — unless the consignee segregates them.

8 · In-bond · Part 18 · CBP Form 7512

Memorize by asking: where does the cargo end? Another U.S. port → 61. Another port then abroad → 62. Abroad from here → 63.

QP withdrawals (FTZ / bonded warehouse). The full bill or pro-bill of the withdrawal must be provided — never the original import bill. All carriers are treated as non-automated, so the bill is not validated against a manifest. The FTZ withdrawal indicator in QP10 must be set and zone fields populated. The FIRMS code of the zone or warehouse may replace the carrier's SCAC where the carrier has none or it is unknown.
Operational note. "Bill not on file" on a withdrawal usually means the withdrawal indicator was not set — the system fell back to manifest validation. The bill is the symptom, not the cause.

9 · Bonded warehouse · Parts 19 and 144

  • Merchandise may remain 5 years from date of importation
  • Duty rate is the one in effect at withdrawal, not at entry (141.69)
  • Withdrawal for consumption = type 31; with quota = 32; with AD/CVD = 34
  • Class 3 = public warehouse; Class 2 = private; Class 6 = manufacturing; Class 8 = cleaning/repacking; Class 11 = general order
  • Permit file folder or activity summary report delivered to CBP after final withdrawal
  • Direct identification allows storage in multiple locations if the inventory system identifies every location and quantity per entry

10 · Foreign trade zones · Part 146

  • Admission on CBP Form 214 / e214 — this creates the zone record everything else references
  • Privileged foreign status locks the classification and rate at the time of the PF election; non-privileged foreign is appraised at withdrawal condition; domestic; zone-restricted (export/destruction only)
  • Withdrawal for consumption = entry type 06
  • Under direct delivery, the operator verifies seals, signs the in-bond/cartage document assuming responsibility under the FTZ Operator's Bond, and forwards the documentation to the port director
  • Deactivation of a site requires a written application by the grantee — an oral request is not sufficient

11 · Classification and the GRIs

The GRIs apply in sequence. You move to the next only when the previous does not resolve. Skipping a step is the most common exam error — and the wrong option is usually the answer a later GRI would give.

GRI 1 — the text governs

Classification is determined by the terms of the headings and any relative section and chapter notes. Table of contents, index and section/chapter titles have no legal force.

GRI 2

2(a) — an incomplete or unfinished article is classified as complete if it has the essential character of the complete article; also covers goods presented unassembled or disassembled.

2(b) — a reference to a material includes mixtures and combinations with other materials; goods of more than one material go to GRI 3.

GRI 3

3(a) — the most specific description prevails. Exception: where two or more headings each refer to only part of the materials in a mixture or part of the items in a set, they are equally specific → go to 3(b).

3(b) — mixtures, composite goods and sets put up for retail sale: classify by the component giving the essential character (nature, bulk, weight, value, or role in use — no fixed formula).

3(c) — remaining ties: the heading last in numerical order among those equally meriting consideration.

GRI 4 — most akin

Where no heading applies, classify under the heading for the goods to which they are most akin. Extremely rare — if you land here in an exam question, you probably erred earlier.

GRI 5 — cases and packing

5(a) — cases specially shaped or fitted, suitable for long-term use, presented with the article, follow the article (unless the container gives the essential character).

5(b) — packing materials follow the goods, except where clearly suitable for repetitive use.

GRI 6 — one level down

The same rules apply at subheading level, mutatis mutandis. Golden rule: only subheadings at the same level are comparable — one dash with one dash, two dashes with two dashes.

Additional U.S. Rules of Interpretation

  • Principal use — controlled by the use in the U.S., at or immediately prior to the date of importation, of goods of that same class or kind. Not the use this importer will make
  • Actual use — intended at importation, goods so used, and proof within 3 years
  • Parts — a "parts" or "parts and accessories" provision does not prevail over a specific provision for that part
  • Textiles — "of a particular textile material" = the material that predominates by weight
Vocabulary. Prima facie classifiable = at first sight the heading appears to cover the goods; several headings may be prima facie applicable at once, which is exactly what triggers GRI 3. It does not mean "correctly classified."

Column 2 countries — General Note 3(b)

Column 2 rates apply to products of countries not entitled to normal trade relations (NTR) treatment. These rates are the old Smoot-Hawley rates and are dramatically higher than Column 1 — often several times the general rate.

CountryNote

Verify this list on exam day. Column 2 membership is set by statute and changes — Russia and Belarus were added in 2022. Read General Note 3(b) in the HTSUS edition in force for your sitting; that text governs, not this table.
Where this bites. A question giving a country of origin plus a component from a Column 2 country is testing whether you apply the origin rules first, then read the correct column. Also: when an absolute quota is closed, "enter and pay Column 2 rates" is never the right answer — Column 2 has nothing to do with quota.

GSP status

The Generalized System of Preferences (SPI "A", "A*", "A+") has lapsed and requires Congressional renewal. When lapsed, importers pay the general Column 1 rate; historically, retroactive refunds have been granted upon renewal. Confirm current status before the exam.

12 · Valuation · Part 152

Hierarchy — in order, no skipping

  1. Transaction value of the imported merchandise
  2. Transaction value of identical merchandise
  3. Transaction value of similar merchandise
  4. Deductive value
  5. Computed value
  6. Derived (fallback) value

Deductive and computed may be reversed at the importer's request.

Transaction value = price actually paid or payable, PLUS

  • Packing costs incurred by the buyer
  • Selling commissions incurred by the buyer
  • Assists, apportioned
  • Royalties or license fees the buyer must pay as a condition of sale
  • Proceeds of subsequent resale accruing to the seller
Buying commissions are NOT added. Selling commissions are. This single distinction appears on nearly every exam.

Assists (152.102(a))

Materials/components incorporated; tools, dies, moulds used in production; merchandise consumed in production; and engineering, development, artwork, design work, plans and sketches undertaken OUTSIDE the United States.

The U.S. engineering exception. Design or engineering work performed in the United States is not an assist. This is the standard question: a drawing made by a U.S. firm → not added.

When transaction value cannot be used

  • Restrictions on disposition or use — except restrictions imposed by law, limiting the geographic area of resale, or not substantially affecting value
  • Sale or price subject to conditions for which a value cannot be determined
  • Proceeds accrue to seller and cannot be adjusted
  • Related parties, unless the relationship did not influence the price or the value closely approximates a test value
Geographic resale restriction. "May only be resold in New York" is an allowed restriction — transaction value still applies. And a buyer's own promotional spending is not added.

Computed value

Cost of materials and processing + profit and general expenses + packing costs + assists and engineering not already included.

13 · Country of origin marking · Part 134

  • Must be legible, indelible and permanent (134.41)
  • In English, in a conspicuous place, so the ultimate purchaser can find it
  • Marking duty: 10% of final appraised value if not properly marked and not exported or destroyed before liquidation (134.51)
  • Acceptable formats: "Made in X", "Product of X", "Assembled in X"
Assembly. Where origin is the country of final assembly, listing several countries as if all were origin ("Product of Vietnam, Thailand and China") is incorrect. "Assembled in Vietnam from components of Thailand and China" is correct.

14 · Quota · Part 132

  • Absolute quota — a hard quantity limit; once filled, no more may enter for consumption
  • Tariff-rate quota — an in-quota rate up to a quantity; above it, a higher rate applies
  • Quota priority — the precedence of one entry over another for the same quota
  • Quota status — the right of the merchandise to the quota benefit

When an absolute quota is closed, the lawful options are: warehouse entry and hold until the quota reopens, place in an FTZ, or export/destroy. Entering at "column 2 rates" is not an option.

15 · AD/CVD and EAPA · Parts 159 and 165

  • Antidumping — foreign merchandise sold in the U.S. at less than fair value, causing or threatening material injury
  • Countervailing — foreign government subsidy
  • Sunset review — not later than every 5 years; would dumping/subsidy likely continue or recur if the order were revoked?
  • Reimbursement certification — the importer must file it before liquidation
  • Commerce sets the rates; the ITC determines injury; CBP collects

EAPA (Part 165): on reasonable suspicion of evasion, CBP takes interim measures — suspends liquidation of unliquidated entries entered on or after initiation, extends liquidation for those entered before, and notifies the parties within 5 business days. Investigations are conducted by the Trade Remedy Law Enforcement Directorate (TRLED).

16 · Drawback · Part 190

  • Manufacturing drawback — 1313(a) direct identification; 1313(b) substitution
  • Unused merchandise — 1313(j)(1) direct identification; 1313(j)(2) substitution
  • Rejected merchandise — 1313(c)
  • Claim filed within 5 years of the date of importation
  • Refund is 99% of duties, taxes and fees
  • Proof of export or destruction is required for a complete claim

17 · Protests, penalties and liquidated damages

  • Protest — 180 days from liquidation. Protestable: classification, rate, value, exclusion, liquidation/reliquidation, and refusal to reliquidate
  • Not protestable — decisions to remit/mitigate a penalty, or to cancel a liquidated damages claim on payment of a lesser amount
  • Penalties (1592) — fraud / gross negligence / negligence, with different maxima; prior disclosure sharply reduces exposure but must precede knowledge of a formal investigation
  • Liquidated damages — contractual, under the bond. Petition within 60 days to the Fines, Penalties and Forfeitures Officer
Liquidation ≠ liquidated damages. Liquidation closes the entry's account. Liquidated damages is the bond's contractual penalty. Different instruments, different deadlines, different paths.

18 · Intellectual property · Part 133

  • Trademarks and copyrights are recorded with CBP for border enforcement
  • Counterfeit goods (133.21) — seizure and civil penalty
  • Gray market (parallel imports) — genuine goods outside the authorized channel. Under the common control exception they normally enter
  • Lever-rule protection — where the foreign article is physically and materially different from the authorized U.S. article, CBP may bar it. Not automatic: the owner must request it and CBP publishes the determination in the Customs Bulletin. A conspicuous permanent label disclosing the difference permits entry
  • Detention of suspected counterfeits: the importer has a limited number of business days to respond before CBP may release unredacted information to the mark owner

19 · USMCA · Part 182

  • Regional value content — two methods: transaction value and net cost
  • Certification of origin — may be completed by importer, exporter or producer; blanket up to 12 months; no prescribed form, but required data elements
  • Post-importation claim — within 1 year of importation (182.32)
  • De minimis — generally 10% (different for textiles)
  • Repairs: goods originating outside the USMCA territory are not eligible for duty-free treatment on return after repair in Canada/Mexico

20 · Recordkeeping · Part 163

  • 5 years from the date of entry (drawback: 3 years from payment of the claim)
  • (a)(1)(A) list — the records required to enter merchandise
  • Penalties for failure to produce: willful vs negligent, with different maxima per release
  • Recordkeeping Compliance Program — certification can mitigate penalties

21 · Fees · 19 CFR 24.23

  • MPF (Merchandise Processing Fee) — formal entries: 0.3464% ad valorem, subject to a minimum and maximum that are adjusted annually. Informal entries pay a flat fee
  • HMF (Harbor Maintenance Fee) — 0.125%, ocean shipments only — not air, not truck, not rail
  • MPF is not charged on certain preference-program goods
Confirm current figures. MPF minimum/maximum change annually by Federal Register notice. Verify the values in effect for your exam date.

22 · Restricted and prohibited merchandise

  • Switchblades — importation prohibited (15 USC 1241–1245); exceptions in 1244 include federal/state/local government entities and a blade of 3 inches or less for a person with one arm
  • Counterfeit currency, plates and dies — seized and delivered to the U.S. Secret Service
  • Endangered species — U.S. Fish & Wildlife; antiques may enter only at designated ports
  • Milk and cream — Federal Import Milk Act requires a permit from HHS
  • White phosphorus matches — prohibited
  • Seditious/treasonous matter — prohibited
Prohibited ≠ restricted. Prohibited merchandise cannot enter at all absent a statutory exception. Restricted merchandise enters with the right permit or licence from a PGA.

23 · Exam-day tactics

  • Tab your books before the exam. The HTSUS runs past 3,000 pages; without tabs you cannot find a chapter in time. Tab the GRIs and General Notes first — you will use them constantly
  • Read the call of the question last. Look for EXCEPT, NOT and FALSE — a large share of missed questions are correct answers to the inverted question
  • In classification, work the GRIs in order and write which one decided it. If you used GRI 3(c) without first exhausting 3(a) and 3(b), re-check
  • In date questions, write the timeline out. Arrival, date of entry, entry summary, liquidation. Then count
  • Calendar days vs working days vs business days — the distinction decides several questions per sitting
  • Do not chase the perfect answer on the first pass. Mark and return. Every question is worth the same
  • Passing score: 75% — 60 of 80

This is a study aid, not a source of law. Every figure, deadline and rule must be confirmed against the current text of 19 CFR, the HTSUS in force on the examination date, and the Notice of Examination for your sitting. Amounts adjusted for inflation (penalties, MPF, bond minimums) change by Federal Register notice and are the most likely items here to be out of date.
